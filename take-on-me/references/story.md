# From idea to shot list

## The shape

The 1985 video is a portal story: someone in the everyday world is drawn — literally — into a pencil world, finds wonder, then trouble, and escapes home changed. Keep that skeleton and hang the user's idea on it. Scale the beats to DURATION; at 20 s each is one or two shots.

| Beat | World | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Home, and a want | toon | One character, one clear desire, shown by action (the cat scrapes an empty bowl). |
| 2. The portal wakes | toon + pencil | An ordinary surface turns to graphite: a bowl's bottom, a comic page, a mirror, a puddle, a phone screen. A pencil thing appears on it — a reflection, a beckoning hand. Curiosity. |
| 3. Crossing | toon → pencil | Pulled, tipped, or fallen in. Quick, physical, funny. |
| 4. The other side | pencil | Wide establishing shot. The character, now a sketch, checks its own hands. The thing it wanted is here. |
| 5. Trouble | pencil | Taking it wakes the locals. Pursuers turn, slowly. One raises a tool. |
| 6. Chase → dead end | pencil | Corridors of ruled lines and panel frames whipping past. Two angles (side tracking, head-on). Then a blank wall. |
| 7. The tear | pencil + light | Clawing at the paper rips a ragged gash; warm light from home pours through — the only colour in this world. |
| 8. Home | toon | Bursts back out of the portal, full colour again. |
| 9. The souvenir | toon + pencil | What came back is still a pencil sketch. A beat. Then the button: the pursuers, crammed behind the portal's surface, pounding on it. Held final image, ≥ 1 s. |

Not every idea is a chase. The invariants are: **a want → a crossing → a reversal on the other side → a way back → something pencil that stays.** A love story, a rescue, or a quiet loss all fit. The direction can flip too (a sketch character escapes into the colour world).

## Turning the idea into pictures

1. **Find the physical world.** List 8–10 concrete, drawable things: objects, tools, places, gestures, creatures. Abstract ideas get metaphors made of physical things. Prefer strong silhouettes and clear light/shadow.
2. **Choose the portal** from the character's everyday life, and decide what leaks through it each way.
3. **Cast small.** One protagonist, one or two pursuers or companions sharing a single rig, one prop that carries the want. Each is a rig of simple solid forms that can be drawn in either world.
4. **People stay stylised and partly hidden** — silhouettes, backs, hands, helmets, goggles, coats, small in frame. A face is a few confident marks (brow shadow, nose line, mouth), never a portrait. Animals and objects are easier and funnier than humans.
5. **One idea per shot.** Vary framing every cut: wide → close → detail → reverse. If a shot needs explaining, replace it.
6. **Steal from anime and silent comedy** for the acting beats: the look before the pounce, focus lines, the freeze mid-bite, the slow turn.

## The brief

Model it on [../example/fish-heist/brief.md](../example/fish-heist/brief.md):

- Title and a two-line premise (format, duration, silent).
- The two worlds and the crossover rule for *this* film (what is pencil inside colour, what is colour inside pencil).
- Shot table: `# · time · world · framing · what happens`. Cuts on 0.5 s marks. World is `toon`, `pencil`, or a mix.
- Status checklist (pass 1 / 2 / 3) and open decisions, kept current as you work.
